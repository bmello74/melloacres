The flock

Geese, goslings and hatching eggs

Three goose breeds, Bresse chickens, and a mixed laying flock of ducks and hens. Everything is hatched and brooded here on the place.

Geese

We keep three breeds, each for a different reason, and sell both hatching eggs and goslings from all of them.

Sebastopol

The long, curled, ribbon-like feathers are what people come for. Calm birds, and unmistakable on the water.

Embden

Big, white and fast-growing — the classic farmyard goose, and the one most people picture when they picture a goose.

Toulouse

Heavy grey birds with a steady, easygoing temperament. Good, reliable setters.

On goslings: we don't sell them at a day old — they're too young to go. Goslings leave here started, once they're up and eating well and can handle the move. If you want them younger than that, take hatching eggs instead.

Before you set eggs

Both guides →

If this is your first hatch, read the guides first — they're free, and they cover the things that actually decide whether eggs hatch: holding the temperature, getting the humidity right, turning on schedule, and leaving the lid shut once the chicks start pipping.

Fresh eggs picked up here hatch at 80–90%. Shipped eggs run lower — that's true of anyone's eggs, and it's worth knowing going in.
