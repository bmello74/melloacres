The flowers

Dahlias, cut fresh

Flowers by the stem and by the bunch through the season, and tubers when the clumps come out of the ground. Everything here is seasonal — but the season in Hanford runs longer than most growers get.

Cut flowers

Cut in the morning and ready the same day. Single stems, mixed bunches, or a bucket if you're doing your own arranging.

Fresh blooms

Whatever is open in the patch that week. What's coming on changes constantly through the season — that's the nature of dahlias.

Tubers

Divided from our own clumps after the season ends, so you can grow the same varieties in your own ground.

A longer season than most

Dahlias are a summer-into-fall flower nearly everywhere. Here in the Central Valley the warm weather holds on well past when other growers have cut their last stems.

That means blooms are available over a longer stretch of the year than you'd get from a grower in a cooler climate — and it means the answer to "do you have flowers right now?" is worth asking, even late in the year.

Tubers work the other way around: they come out of the ground after the plants are done, get divided, and go out ahead of the next planting season. Cut flowers and tubers are never available at the same moment.
